# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in sugars. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts, organelles that contain the green pigment chlorophyll.

The process has two stages. The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes, where chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and splits water molecules, releasing oxygen and producing energy carriers. The light-independent reactions, known as the Calvin cycle, take place in the stroma and use that captured energy to fix carbon dioxide into glucose.

Photosynthesis underpins almost all life on Earth. It supplies the oxygen that most organisms breathe and forms the base of nearly every food chain by turning inorganic carbon into the organic molecules that other organisms consume.
